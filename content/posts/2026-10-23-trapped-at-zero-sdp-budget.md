# Transitioning to SDP: Addressing the "Zero Baseline Budget" Challenge

> **From Our Independent Facilitator Interviews:** A Person-Centered Plan (PCP) is not just a mechanical conversion of an IPP. When a family transitions to SDP after a year of sitting on agency waitlists, deeply documenting the child's holistic needs and unmet services is essential to building a realistic initial budget.

When a family decides to transition into California's Self-Determination Program (SDP), the Regional Center calculates an initial **12-month Individual Budget** based by default on the total Purchase of Service (POS) dollars spent on the participant over the prior 12 months.

Here is the catch: if your child spent the last 12 months sitting on waitlists for vendored speech, occupational therapy, or respite agencies, the Regional Center may have spent `$0`—producing an initial baseline calculation that does not reflect your child's actual needs.

---

## Documenting Unmet Needs Under the Lanterman Act

The Lanterman Act provides mechanisms to adjust an SDP budget when historical spending does not reflect newly identified or previously unmet needs:

1. **Preserve Waitlist & Outreach Evidence:** Keep a dated log in your **Vault** showing every vendored provider contacted, their reported waitlist length, or their inability to staff authorized hours.
2. **Invest in a Holistic Person-Centered Plan (PCP):** Work with an Independent Facilitator to write a PCP that captures the whole child—strengths, daily family reality, teacher and therapist input, and concrete life goals—rather than merely copying boilerplate IPP lines.
3. **Map Unmet Needs to Service Codes and Real Burdened Costs:** Translate each needed support into its corresponding Regional Center service code, hourly rate, and annual hours so your budget change request is backed by transparent math before your IPP/SDP budget certification meeting.
